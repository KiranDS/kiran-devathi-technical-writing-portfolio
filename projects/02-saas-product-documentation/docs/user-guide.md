# SupportFlow user guide

**Audience:** Support agents and team leads  
**Product version:** 1.0

## 1. Purpose

This guide explains common support workflows in the fictional SupportFlow application. It focuses on finding work, updating tickets, collaborating with a team, and resolving customer requests.

## 2. Work from your inbox

The Inbox is the illustrative view of tickets assigned to your account.

1. Open **Inbox**.
2. Review tickets by priority and age, following your team's operating procedure.
3. Select a ticket to inspect its details.
4. Review the requester, description, activity history, and current status.
5. Confirm that you are the assignee before updating the ticket.

Do not assume that the oldest or highest-priority ticket must always be handled first; follow the priority and escalation policy defined by your organisation.

## 3. Work from a queue

Queues provide shared views of tickets.

1. Open **Queues**.
2. Select the relevant team queue.
3. Filter by status, priority, or assignee when those filters are available.
4. Open a ticket and review its history.
5. Assign or claim the ticket according to team policy.

A queue filter changes the list displayed; it does not change the underlying ticket.

## 4. Update a ticket

1. Open the ticket.
2. Review the latest activity.
3. Update the status, priority, or assignee as appropriate.
4. Add a clear internal note or customer-facing response, using the correct communication type.
5. Save the changes.
6. Confirm that the updated values appear on the ticket.

**Writing guidance:** Record actions taken, evidence reviewed, and the next expected step. Avoid vague notes such as “looked into it.”

## 5. Use Pending status

Use Pending when the next action depends on information or work outside the support team's immediate control, for example, a response from the requester.

Before changing the status:
- Record what information is needed.
- Identify who is expected to provide it.
- Communicate the next step to the requester where appropriate.
- Follow the team's policy for reminders and follow-up.

The effect of Pending on SLA timers depends on the configured policy. Do not assume that all SLA clocks pause automatically.

## 6. Resolve a ticket

1. Confirm that the reported issue has been addressed or that the agreed resolution has been provided.
2. Record the resolution in the ticket.
3. Add any relevant reference to a knowledge article.
4. Change the status to **Resolved**.
5. Save the ticket.
6. Confirm that the ticket appears as Resolved.

Do not resolve a ticket solely to remove it from a queue.

## 7. Maintain useful ticket records

A useful ticket record should include:
- A specific subject.
- A concise description of the reported issue.
- Reproducible steps or relevant context, when available.
- The impact on the requester.
- The current owner and status.
- Investigation notes and customer communications.
- A clear resolution or next action.

Avoid adding passwords, access tokens, payment-card data, or other secrets to ticket descriptions and notes.

## 8. Team lead workflow

Team leads can use shared queues to:
- Review unassigned work.
- Identify tickets approaching SLA targets.
- Reassign work when capacity or expertise requires it.
- Review tickets that have remained Pending.
- Monitor team workload and service trends.

Use the [Reporting and analytics](reporting-and-analytics.md) guide for metric definitions.
